01 Who we are

Holding company by structure. Builder by nature.

Powerhouse Holdings, LLC is the company that owns and operates all of our ventures. Where a traditional holding company allocates capital across businesses it acquires, Powerhouse allocates something scarcer — engineering conviction — across businesses it creates. Every platform in the portfolio was conceived here, architected here, built here, and is run here.

The model exists for a reason. Software built by an agency gets handed off; software built by a venture studio gets spun out; software built inside a holding company gets lived with. When the builder is also the long-term operator, quality stops being a deliverable and becomes self-interest. Architectural shortcuts have nowhere to hide, because we're the ones who will meet them again at 2 a.m.

The portfolio deliberately spans industries — personal productivity, consumer finance, live commerce — because the house's real asset isn't any single market. It's a repeatable way of building: modular systems that share contracts rather than tables, analytics that measure rather than assume, and automation that drafts while a human decides.

That same asset is retained by others. A private advisory practice serves organisations from government contractors to the private sector — much of it under NDA, all of it held to the house standard. The quietest work in the portfolio is some of the work we're proudest of.

02 The operating model

How a venture moves
through the house.

Four stages, applied identically to every platform in the portfolio — whatever the industry.

/ 01

Observe

Every venture starts with an inefficiency we've felt firsthand: a market where the data evaporates, a process that thrives on opacity, a tool category that forgot its user. We write down the mechanics of the domain before writing code — and keep that document honest as reality corrects it.

Domain first
/ 02

Build

Architecture before features. A kernel of pure logic, modules behind versioned contracts, entitlements and privacy designed in from the first commit. Minimal dependencies, no build-step archaeology — systems a single disciplined team can hold in its head for a decade.

Contracts, not tables
/ 03

Operate

The house runs what it builds — deployment, monitoring, support, and the unglamorous day-two work that separates products from demos. Operating our own systems is where the next round of product truths comes from.

Day two is the product
/ 04

Compound

Learning loops make each system better every week it runs — measured signal weights, learned aliases, tuned thresholds. And what one venture learns, the house keeps: the credit platform reads the calendar platform's dates; the market platform's kernel discipline shapes everything after it.

The house keeps what it learns

03 The standard

What "built by Powerhouse" means.

Engineering

  • — Boundaries verified at boot, not discovered in production
  • — Breaking changes version the contract, loudly
  • — Dependencies earn their place or stay out
  • — Every empty state names what fills it; no control does nothing

Data

  • — Local-first where possible, consent-gated where not
  • — Accuracy measured per signal, never assumed
  • — Every learned rule records its origin and can be revoked
  • — Zero telemetry that doesn't serve the owner of the data

Judgment

  • — Automation drafts; a human with standing decides
  • — Authority is earned — three observers equal one operator
  • — Provisional answers arrive in seconds and say so
  • — Systems refuse to tune themselves on noise

Leadership

Founder-led,
founder-run.

Powerhouse Holdings was founded and is led by Matthew Martin, who remains the principal architect across the portfolio. The house is deliberately small: decisions travel at the speed of conviction, and the person accountable for a system's promises is the person who designed them.

That structure is a feature clients and partners feel directly — there is no layer between the standard and the person who set it.
