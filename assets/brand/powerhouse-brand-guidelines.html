01 The idea

Contained energy.

The name promises power. The brand's discipline is to hold it — the way a holding company should.

The identity is built on one motif: the charged seam — a single precise bolt of negative space cut through a solid plate. Energy, held in structure. It appears as the cut in the mark, as the vertical seam of light on hero surfaces, and nowhere else.

Everything around the seam stays quiet on purpose: near-black grounds, warm bone paper, hairline rules, generous space. The restraint is the message — a company confident enough not to shout.

This edition supersedes the original consumer-facing identity (electric lattice, saturated ultraviolet-and-lime). That energy survives here, matured: the violet deepened into a structural accent, the lime retired from corporate use, the voltage expressed through precision instead of glow. The legacy logotype is retained only in product-community contexts that predate this edition.

03 Colour

Obsidian carries. Violet signs.

Proportion keeps the palette professional: roughly 70% ground, 20% paper, 8% structure, 2% signature.

Obsidian

#0A0A0C

The ground. Carries the brand; most surfaces live here.

Ink

#101116

Raised surfaces and panels on Obsidian.

Bone

#F2F0EC

The paper. Warm inversion sections and print grounds.

Ultraviolet

#6F5BE8

The signature. Seams, focus, selection — structural, rarely filled.

Ultraviolet Deep

#3B2F86

Ceremonial grounds and gradient depth.

Brass

#C6A972

Premium emphasis. At most one element per view.

Always

  • Hold the 70 / 20 / 8 / 2 proportion across any composition.
  • Use Ultraviolet for structure — rules, seams, focus states — before fills.
  • Check text contrast: body copy on Obsidian is Bone at 92%+, never violet.
  • Let hairlines and spacing do the separating before colour does.

Never

  • Introduce colours outside this palette — including the retired lime.
  • Place Ultraviolet text on Obsidian at body sizes.
  • Use gradients except violet-into-darkness on hero surfaces.
  • Use Brass and Ultraviolet as competing accents in one view.

The hierarchy is fixed: serif for what we believe, grotesk for what we explain, mono for what we measure. If a layout needs a fourth voice, the layout is wrong.

05 Voice & motion

Measured. Precise. Unhurried.

Write like the software is built: no filler, no hype, every claim load-bearing.

We sound like

  • "Provisional in two seconds. Settled in four to six."
  • "Nothing leaves the house without a human's approval."
  • "Accuracy is measured, not assumed."

We never sound like

  • "Revolutionary AI-powered game-changing solutions!!"
  • Exclamation points, superlatives, or urgency theatre.
  • Gamer slang, internet casing, or meme cadence — anywhere.

Motion

Easing. One family: cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1). Long decelerations, no bounce, no elastic. Entrances 0.9–1.25 s; micro-interactions 0.35–0.6 s.

Vocabulary. Masked line reveals for display type, soft rises for content, drawn rules, receding heroes, stacking plates. One seam of light per view — motion obeys the same scarcity as colour.

Respect. Reduced-motion preferences collapse every effect to instant, fully legible states. The document must read perfectly with no JavaScript at all.

Where it lives. The reference implementation is powerhouseholdings.us — its stylesheet (assets/css/site.css) is the canonical token source for digital work.

06 Stewardship

The brand is held, too.

These guidelines apply to the Powerhouse Holdings parent brand. Portfolio products — ChronoCal, Scorely, Cardonomics — carry their own product identities, each designed to sit comfortably beside the parent without imitating it.

Questions of application not covered here are settled by one test: does it look like a company that would build our software? If the answer is uncertain, it is no.
